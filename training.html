27 · The training — where the habit was built

The race page covers 65 days out of fifteen years. This one covers the other thing entirely: every training run of 25 miles or more in the Strava export — 119 of them, none of which had a bib, a clock, or anyone watching. They were never logged as negative-split attempts, which is exactly what makes them interesting. If pacing is a skill rather than a race-day decision, it should show up here first, and it should show up as a habit that strengthens over time. Every long run is included, not just the good ones: a rate needs a denominator.

Caveats. These runs were not scored by anyone at the time, so there is no stated intent to succeed or fail against — a negative split here may be a plan, a warm-up mile, or a downhill route home, and the terrain toggle only removes the third of those. Training runs also have no official distance to check the GPS against, so the loop-course drift that the race page can catch is invisible here. Twelve files carry no usable altitude and cannot be adjusted at all; they are marked no alt. The floor of 25 miles is the owner's, and it means this is a page about long runs, not about training as a whole — the speed work, the doubles and the recovery weeks that make the long runs possible are all outside it.
