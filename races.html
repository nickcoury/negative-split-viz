26 · Every race — the full career

The attempt log presents these 65 races as the athlete wrote them down: two half-times per row, typed into a spreadsheet, to the minute. This page re-measures every one of them from the GPS track the athlete was actually wearing — 60 of the 65 — and then asks the question the clock cannot answer: how much of each split was pacing, and how much was the hill? Most of these races are on mountain trail. A 50k whose first half climbs 1,400 ft and whose second half gives it all back is scored a negative split by any stopwatch, and that number means nothing. Flip the toggle and see which ones survive.

Chart 26.2 — What the terrain was hiding

Clock split against terrain-adjusted split. Points on the diagonal are races where the ground was symmetric and the adjustment changes nothing. Points below the diagonal ran the harder half second and are better than they look; points above it got a downhill finish. Anything in a shaded quadrant changed sign — a negative split that was really the hill, or a positive one that was really a climb. Marker size is climb per kilometre; hover for the course profile.

Chart 26.3 — Does the skill survive the adjustment?

Share of races with a faster second half, by class, measured both ways. If the negative splits were an artefact of downhill finishes, the adjusted bars would collapse. Read the gap between each pair as the amount of credit the terrain was giving.

Chart 26.4 — Inside one race

Pick any race with a track. The upper trace is pace per kilometre — solid where it is raw, dashed where it is terrain-adjusted; the shaded band underneath is the course elevation. The vertical line is the halfway point that defines the split. This is where a "negative split" turns out to be a 300 m descent.

What the flags mean. gps the GPS distance disagrees with the official race distance by more than 3% — on a short looped course the drift is uneven between halves, which is enough to invent or erase a split, and there the official lap count is the better instrument. sheet the track and the spreadsheet disagree by more than 1.5 points. no alt the file carries no usable altitude, so the adjusted number is just the raw one. Two 2011/2017 races predate or are missing from the export entirely and have no track at all.
And what this page still is. One athlete, self-selected races, and the terrain model is a model — Minetti's cost-of-running curve, not a measurement of this runner's own economy on this ground. It agrees with Strava's independent implementation to a mean of 0.8% with no bias as the ground steepens, which makes it good enough to separate a 3,600 m mountain race from a flat one; it is not good enough to rank two efforts a fraction of a percent apart. None of these rows are in the research database, and none of them are evidence about anybody else.
