How rare are negative and even splits among elite ultrarunners, and do world-class efforts pace more evenly? These pages explore a research dataset built from official results and lap data. Work in progress — findings are preliminary and the full analysis is not yet published.

The argument, in order

Seven pages that build the paper's case for a skeptical reader — each one states its own caveats. Start here.

  • 17 · The Census
    Negative splits are real but rare, and mostly accidents. Every lap-resolved negative/even 24h split given a verdict: held back · metronome · overcooked · stop&recover — the 46 split 5/12/22/7.
  • 19 · The marathon mirror
    The assumption gap is real. Verified WR splits (all within ~1% of even) and the published pacing literature vs this dataset's +14% median elite fade.
  • 18 · Your best day is your most even
    Within careers, evenness tracks peak performance. 590 athletes vs themselves; the staircase from +5% splits (75th career percentile) to +30% (13th). What it can and cannot conclude.
  • 21 · Three lenses, and two of them lie
    The confounds, taken seriously. The chart that flatters even pacing, the chart that flatters banking, why each lies — and the matched-pairs design (same race, same halfway distance) where the evener arrival wins 64% by +7 km.
  • 22 · The anatomy of the fade
    The fade is collapse, not destiny. Median 62% of second-half time-loss is discrete bad patches, foreshadowed before halfway; the five deliberate runs never leave ±4% while the field swings 46 points.
  • 20 · Nobody is shooting threes
    The strategy is untested, not disproven. 9 of 3,264 elite full-day efforts ever opened at/below their own final average; six of the nine landed negative or even, none blew up.
  • 23 · N = 1 — the attempt log
    What practicing the skill looks like. 65 self-recorded attempts over 15 years, 62% success, transferring up-distance until the American Record — presented as anecdote and as a template.
  • 24 · The engine room
    Even pace was even effort. Per-mile heart rate from the AR run: −1.6% Pa:HR decoupling over 24 hours, 8.8 min stopped all day; the Dome 100 closed fastest at 17 bpm below its opening.
  • 25 · The experiment nobody has run
    The protocol, not a verdict. The paper's five claims, and the four asks: attempt logs with pre-declared plans, published lap data, a season-long cohort study, and the three-point long game.
