Exploration 16 · Intentional vs accidental negative splits

A negative split can happen two very different ways. Intentional: hold back on purpose — the foresight line (what you're on pace for) sits below your eventual average for most of the race and only rises to meet it late. Accidental: go out ahead of final pace, something goes wrong, and the recovery happens to make the second half faster. Identical sign of split_pct, different class of performance.
All three lines are put on one scale: x = % of race completed, y = % ahead of your own final average pace (+ = on pace for a better result than you got, − = conservative). The metric is the area between each line and zero, split above/below: score = (area below − area above) / total, in [−1, +1]. It is computed for all three lines so they can be compared (chart 16.3), but only the foresight score is used to call a performance intentional or accidental: foresight score > 0 = mostly conservative → looks intentional; < 0 = mostly ahead → looks accidental. Hindsight is close to a mirror of foresight and instantaneous pace barely varies, so neither carries an independent verdict.
