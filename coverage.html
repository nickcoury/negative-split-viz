Coverage status · what we hold of each rung's all-time top 100

make coverage answers how much of each rung's all-time list we hold splits for. This page answers which ones, and at what fidelity — one row per performance, so "what should we fetch next" is a thing you read rather than derive. Matched on the clock, the sex and the year, never the name (VINCENT LANGAT vs Vincent Kibet LANGAT).

The ratings

weighted = share of sum(1/rank) over the top 100 that we hold splits for — rank 1 alone is 19% of it, the top 10 are 59%, ranks 51–100 are 13%. So it is a caliber score, not a count. en route = covered by a mark set during a longer race rather than in a race run to that distance; counted in its own column because running through a distance is not racing to it. n/a = structurally unsplittable and out of both sides of the fraction.

The performances

Two fidelity axes, and they do not rank the same way — a marathon has the coarsest shape in the dataset and the most precise halfway point in it. halfway is what split_pct depends on: measured means a checkpoint lands on the halfway point, interpolated means it was estimated across the marks that bracket it (the bracket width is shown). shape is the checkpoint cadence as a fraction of the race — lap_complete <1%, sampled 1–6%, coarse 6–20%, halves_only ≥20%; only the first three can carry a pace shape at all.

Where the missing performances are

The same rows grouped by RACE and ranked by the weight they would deliver — this is data/targets/coverage_gaps.csv, read from the top. A race already in the DB with finishing times and no splits is a splits problem, not a discovery one.
