22 · The anatomy of the fade

The strongest argument that 24-hour pacing cannot improve is physiological: "of course the second half is slower — fatigue is not optional." This page dissects the fade to test that. If the fade were mostly a smooth, obligatory slowdown, holding pace would indeed be fantasy. What the lap data shows instead: the elite fade is dominated by discrete collapse — crawl laps and bad patches — and it is foreshadowed hours ahead. A collapse is not an obligation; it is the thing the nine attempters conspicuously avoided.

Chart 22.1 — The shape of everyone's day, and the five who refused it

Hour-by-hour pace vs the athlete's own eventual average, all … full-day lap-resolved efforts (gray mean) vs the five deliberate negative splits (teal). The field starts +28% hot and ends −18% under; the held-back five never leave a ±4% band, and in the closing hours — when the field is deepest underwater — they are the only ones running above their average. Same species, different sport.

Chart 22.2 — What the lost time actually is: crawls, not drift

For every lap-complete positive-split effort, second-half time-loss (vs the athlete's own first-half median lap) is split into mild drift (laps up to 1.5× that median — running slower) and bad patches (laps beyond 1.5× — crawling, walking, stopped). The histogram shows the bad-patch share per athlete: the median fade is …% bad patches, and for …% of athletes the patches are the majority of the loss. The fade is not a slope; it is an event.

Chart 22.3 — The collapse is foreshadowed before halfway

Using only first-half information: how much did the athlete slow from their first quarter (0–6h) to their second (6–12h)? Then measure how much of their second half was spent in the crawl regime (laps >1.5× their first-half median). Athletes who held their opening pace through halfway spent ~16% of the second half crawling; athletes already 15%+ slower by halfway spent ~45%. The blow-up announces itself six hours early — which is precisely the window a pacing plan controls.

Reading it honestly

The fade is behavioral enough to be worth attacking. Some slowdown over 24 hours is surely physiologic. But a median fade that is …% discrete bad patches, foreshadowed by first-half behavior, is not a law of nature — it is the signature of pace mismanagement interacting with fatigue. That is exactly the component training and strategy can address, and exactly what the marathon world spent decades learning to engineer out (see the mirror).
Caveats. Lap-complete feeds only for 22.2/22.3 (a sampled hourly feed can't see a crawl lap); the 1.5× threshold is a definition, not a discovery — shift it and the shares move, but the ordering in 22.3 does not. "Crawl" time includes planned stops (aid, kit, night gear): the data cannot distinguish a strategic stop from a collapse, which cuts both ways. Hour curves use each athlete's own average as the reference, so the chart shows pacing shape, not speed.
