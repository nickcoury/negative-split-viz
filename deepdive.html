Exploration 30 · Inside one discipline

Pick a rung of the distance ladder and this page shows nothing else. Every chart is scoped to that one race distance, on the metric the census runs on: seconds of intentionality — the mean gap in s/mi between what an athlete was on pace for at each point and what they finally averaged, with x normalised so race length drops out. Negative = held back early (ran conservative of the pace they ended up averaging); positive = went out ahead of it. Definition and derivation: Exploration 16.

Two things about the rung selector. It groups by the race distance of the EFFORT, not by the event: the 100 miles run inside a 24-hour race sits on the 100-mile rung, which is where a reader looking for a hundred expects it, and the shapes pages' cohort selector splits that rung three ways instead. And the caliber axis is the same one the Ladder uses — the result divided by the reference mark for that rung and sex — so 1.00 is the record and 1.10 is ten percent off it. On the 100-mile and 24-hour rungs there is no ratified record, so the reference is the best mark in this dataset and is labelled as such.

30.1 — The rung's signature: how the pace actually moved

The instantaneous pace over ten race deciles, as a percentage of this athlete's own average — up is faster. Line is the median, the shaded band the interquartile range, and the three colours are the outcome bands so you can see whether a negative split at this distance is a different SHAPE or the same shape shifted. Ten deciles rather than the raw checkpoints on purpose: a decile is 10% of the race at every distance, so this chart is not a picture of how densely the timers mat the course (analysis/cadence_calibration.py, study D).

30.2 — What they were on pace for, all race long

The foresight line: average pace from the gun to each point, in seconds per mile against the pace they finished up averaging. This is the curve intent_s is literally the mean height of. Below zero = conservative so far. Median with a 25–75 and a 10–90 band. Curves start where each athlete's first checkpoint is, so the left edge thins out on rungs whose first mat is late; the count under each x is in the hover.

30.3 — Where this rung sits, and how wide it spreads

The distribution of seconds of intentionality. The dotted line at −1 s/mi is the census threshold for held back on purpose: under a second either way is a runner holding their average, not a plan.

30.4 — Do the best performances at this distance hold back more?

Every performance on the rung: x = result ÷ reference mark (left is closer to the record), y = seconds of intentionality. The white line is the median within each decile of caliber, which is the part to read — the cloud is wide everywhere. A line sloping down to the left means the closer to the record, the more conservative the opening.

30.5 — The verdict mix, and how it moves with caliber

The four-way census taxonomy (Exploration 17) applied to this rung, in caliber quintiles. held back ≤ −1 s/mi · metronome ≤ +5 with no stop · stop & recover a gap long enough to sink the average · overcooked everything else. A rung whose feed is too coarse to see a stop is never offered that verdict at all, and says so.

30.6 — Intentionality against the split it produced

The two metrics side by side. They are not the same thing: split_pct is a comparison of two halves and intent_s is an integral over the whole race, so a race can be negative by a hair and wildly overcooked, or positive and entirely deliberate. The correlation is printed — where it is near −1 the two are saying one thing, and where it is not the split is hiding something.

30.7 — Which tenth of the race was fastest, and which was slowest

Counting, over every performance on the rung, the decile that held the quickest pace and the decile that held the slowest. A rung that opens hard and fades puts fastest at 1 and slowest at 10; a tactical track race puts fastest at 10 and slowest in the middle; a race with a bad patch puts slowest wherever the patch was.

30.8 — Has pacing at this distance changed?

Median seconds of intentionality by year, with the number of performances behind each point. Thin years move a long way on very little, so the marker size is the sample and the shaded band is the interquartile range. This is a coverage curve as much as a history: what we hold for a year is what someone published.
