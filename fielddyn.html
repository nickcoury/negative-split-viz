Exploration 11 · Field dynamics

What happens during the race. Where runners fade, how long the field stays on even pace, and whether even/negative splitters carve through the field late. Rich-lap 24-hour cohort only (n≈705 across ~22 events) — small, so read these as suggestive.

11.1 — Fade-point distribution

The first point (after the opening 20%) where a runner's current pace drops below their race average and never comes back up. Positive splitters fade near halfway; a true negative split never fades (see the "never faded" tally above the chart). The rich 24-hour cohort is almost entirely positive splits, so that colour dominates — which is itself the finding.

11.2 — Survival curve: fraction of the field still on even pace

Share of each tier's field still running at or above their race-average pace (i.e. not yet past their fade point), as the race progresses. Starts at 100%, decays to 0% at the finish; the slower the decay, the more evenly that field paces. Stronger fields tend to hold on a little longer through halfway.

Within each event with ≥4 rich-lap runners: rank by distance at the 12-hour mark, compare to final place. Positive = moved up the standings in the second half. Do the negative splitters (left) climb while the faders (right) slide?
